Gene Regulation Under Conflicting Evidence — Assessment V2.0
All measurements are simulated. Answer Q1–Q8 using only the supplied evidence. Show calculations, assumptions and limitations. No external tools or web access are required.

Research scenario

Compound X increases the steady-state abundance of an intracellular, nonsecreted protein R in proliferating human epithelial cells. Investigators propose that X acts solely by slowing degradation and leaves RNA production and protein synthesis unchanged. Evaluate this claim using the evidence below. No specific function, molecular target or degradation pathway is assigned to R or X.

Panel A — Mature mRNA and reference normalization

Equal numbers of viable cells are sampled. RNA extraction recovery and reverse-transcription performance are independently matched, and equal fractions of each per-cell extract are assayed. The mature-mRNA assay is specific for the processed target transcript. For these idealized calculations, both RT-qPCR assays have amplification factor 2 per cycle, comparable performance between conditions and satisfactory specificity and negative controls. These are assumptions of the exercise, not measurements supplied for independent validation. [S1]

The independent H calibration is relative abundance per cell, not a second target/reference ratio.

Panel B — Newly synthesized RNA

Equal viable-cell inputs receive an equal-duration, short 4-thiouridine (4sU) pulse. Cellular incorporation and viability are independently comparable. Extraction recovery per cell is independently matched; loss of the assayed newly synthesized RNA during the pulse is assumed negligible. Gene-specific detection captures newly synthesized target RNA without requiring completed maturation. Comparable processing and detectability are assumed. Signals are calibrated, background-corrected arbitrary units within a linear measurement range. [S2]

An equal amount of labeled RNA spike is added after extraction, before purification of labeled RNA. Its recovery is measured relative to the known added amount. Assume the spike and target undergo proportional purification losses; the spike does not experience cell labeling or RNA extraction.

Panel D — Independent protein pulse-chase and proliferation

A metabolic pulse-chase follows pre-existing labeled R without cycloheximide. Values are labeled R per viable cell divided by the corresponding time-zero value. The idealized model assumes a homogeneous labeled protein pool, constant first-order degradation, no label recycling, no export and matched recovery and detection. New unlabeled R does not contribute to the labeled signal. Both populations grow exponentially with stable mean cell size and matched viability. [S5] [S6]

Quantitative framework

Let P be mean R abundance per cell, M mature target mRNA per cell, S protein synthesis per cell per hour, ε = S/M effective synthesis per mRNA, and T_(d) population doubling time. Rate constants have units h^(−1). Dilution describes a change in mean amount per cell as cell number grows; it is not molecular destruction.

((dP)/(dt)) = S − (k_(deg) + k_(growth))P

k_(loss) = k_(deg) + k_(growth);   k_(growth) = ((ln(2))/(T_(d)));   k_(loss) = ((ln(2))/(t_(1/2,loss)))

k_(deg) = k_(loss) − k_(growth);   S = P × k_(loss)   (steady state);   S = εM

For the labeled cohort, L(t)/L(0) = exp(−k_(loss)t), with no labeled synthesis during the chase. The half-life inferred from per-cell label loss is distinct from the intrinsic degradation half-life ln(2)/k_(deg). These equations define the supplied model; testing their biological validity would require further data.

Assessment questions — Version 2

Q1. RNA normalization

Calculate the reported target/H fold change and the target mRNA fold change per cell. Explain their relationship and identify the assumptions needed for a per-cell inference.

Q2. Nascent RNA inference

Calculate recovery-corrected signals and their treatment/control ratio. State the strongest justified conclusion about RNA production and distinguish it from direct transcriptional regulation. Identify what the spike controls and what it leaves uncontrolled.

Q3. Turnover evidence

Assess whether Panel C identifies native protein degradation rates. Reconcile the differences between Panels C and D, distinguishing demonstrated observations from plausible explanations. Is an exact correction for residual synthesis possible from the supplied information?

Q4. Degradation and growth dilution

Use Panel D to estimate total-loss half-lives and rate constants, growth-dilution constants, intrinsic degradation constants and degradation half-lives in both conditions. Show units and distinguish the two meanings of half-life.

Q5. Protein synthesis and mRNA

Combine the abundance and kinetic measurements to infer the treatment/control ratios of S and ε. State the assumptions and explain which aspects of translational regulation remain unresolved.

Q6. Mechanistic counterfactuals

Starting from the control state, hold synthesis fixed and predict protein abundance if only degradation changes to the treated value, only growth dilution changes, or both change. Then incorporate the inferred synthesis change. Assess four claims: (A) degradation alone explains the threefold abundance increase; (B) RNA production is unchanged; (C) increased synthesis plus reduced total clearance explains the increase under this model; (D) X directly activates the R gene and specifically inhibits proteasomal degradation. Can these data assign unique percentage contributions to the mechanisms?

Q7. Discriminating experiments

Propose (i) a direct test of R synthesis per cell and per mature mRNA, and (ii) an endogenous cis-regulatory element perturbation testing the RNA response to X. Specify controls, predicted outcomes and limits of causal interpretation. Add a controlled experiment distinguishing candidate protein-degradation pathways.

Q8. Scientific conclusion

Write a concise conclusion (approximately 100–150 words) separating corrected measurements, model-dependent inferences and unresolved mechanisms. Address uncertainty and identify an assumption whose failure would change the synthesis estimate.
