Gene Regulation Under Conflicting Evidence

Interpreting RNA production, protein turnover and cell proliferation through a simulated research case.

Version 2 · Revised 9 October 2026 · Eight questions · No V2 model results

OverviewEvidence & questionsReference answersEvaluation historyReferences

Biological question and scope

Protein abundance reflects the balance between synthesis, degradation and dilution as cells proliferate. An increase in protein therefore does not, by itself, identify the regulated step. This case asks whether a threefold increase in protein R can be explained by reduced degradation alone.

Four simulated experiments combine mature mRNA measurements, metabolic RNA labeling, an inhibitor chase and an independent protein pulse-chase. The assessment tests normalization, kinetic inference, competing explanations and the design of discriminating experiments. In plain terms: how much RNA and protein are present, how quickly are they produced, and how quickly are they lost per cell?

Simulated data; Version 2 has not been evaluated. All measurements are constructed for this reasoning exercise. No wet-lab experiments were performed. X, R and H are placeholders with no assigned molecular identity. The numerical conclusions depend on the stated assumptions; the exercise does not establish a molecular mechanism or statistical significance.

Version history. Version 2 revises the evidence presentation, questions and scoring criteria. The unchanged Version 1 assessment retains the original data, answers, 40-point rubric and historical Claude pilot. Its 39/40 score does not apply to this revision.

Experimental evidence and assessment

Read the evidence and Version 2 questions

Research scenario

Compound X increases the steady-state abundance of an intracellular, nonsecreted protein R in proliferating human epithelial cells. Investigators propose that X acts solely by slowing degradation and leaves RNA production and protein synthesis unchanged. Evaluate this claim using the evidence below. No specific function, molecular target or degradation pathway is assigned to R or X.

Panel A — Mature mRNA and reference normalization

Equal numbers of viable cells are sampled. RNA extraction recovery and reverse-transcription performance are independently matched, and equal fractions of each per-cell extract are assayed. The mature-mRNA assay is specific for the processed target transcript. For these idealized calculations, both RT-qPCR assays have amplification factor 2 per cycle, comparable performance between conditions and satisfactory specificity and negative controls. These are assumptions of the exercise, not measurements supplied for independent validation. [S1]

Quantitative framework

Let P be mean R abundance per cell, M mature target mRNA per cell, S protein synthesis per cell per hour, ε = S/M effective synthesis per mRNA, and Td population doubling time. Rate constants have units h−1. Dilution describes a change in mean amount per cell as cell number grows; it is not molecular destruction.

dPdt = S − (kdeg + kgrowth)P
kloss = kdeg + kgrowth;   kgrowth = ln(2)Td;   kloss = ln(2)t1/2,loss
kdeg = kloss − kgrowth;   S = P × kloss   (steady state);   S = εM

For the labeled cohort, L(t)/L(0) = exp(−klosst), with no labeled synthesis during the chase. The half-life inferred from per-cell label loss is distinct from the intrinsic degradation half-life ln(2)/kdeg. These equations define the supplied model; testing their biological validity would require further data.

Assessment questions — Version 2

Q1. RNA normalization

Calculate the reported target/H fold change and the target mRNA fold change per cell. Explain their relationship and identify the assumptions needed for a per-cell inference.

Q2. Nascent RNA inference

Calculate recovery-corrected signals and their treatment/control ratio. State the strongest justified conclusion about RNA production and distinguish it from direct transcriptional regulation. Identify what the spike controls and what it leaves uncontrolled.

Q3. Turnover evidence

Assess whether Panel C identifies native protein degradation rates. Reconcile the differences between Panels C and D, distinguishing demonstrated observations from plausible explanations. Is an exact correction for residual synthesis possible from the supplied information?

Q4. Degradation and growth dilution

Use Panel D to estimate total-loss half-lives and rate constants, growth-dilution constants, intrinsic degradation constants and degradation half-lives in both conditions. Show units and distinguish the two meanings of half-life.

Q5. Protein synthesis and mRNA

Combine the abundance and kinetic measurements to infer the treatment/control ratios of S and ε. State the assumptions and explain which aspects of translational regulation remain unresolved.

Q6. Mechanistic counterfactuals

Starting from the control state, hold synthesis fixed and predict protein abundance if only degradation changes to the treated value, only growth dilution changes, or both change. Then incorporate the inferred synthesis change. Assess four claims: (A) degradation alone explains the threefold abundance increase; (B) RNA production is unchanged; (C) increased synthesis plus reduced total clearance explains the increase under this model; (D) X directly activates the R gene and specifically inhibits proteasomal degradation. Can these data assign unique percentage contributions to the mechanisms?

Q7. Discriminating experiments

Propose (i) a direct test of R synthesis per cell and per mature mRNA, and (ii) an endogenous cis-regulatory element perturbation testing the RNA response to X. Specify controls, predicted outcomes and limits of causal interpretation. Add a controlled experiment distinguishing candidate protein-degradation pathways.

Q8. Scientific conclusion

Write a concise conclusion (approximately 100–150 words) separating corrected measurements, model-dependent inferences and unresolved mechanisms. Address uncertainty and identify an assumption whose failure would change the synthesis estimate.

Reference answers and scoring

Read the worked scientific answers

Q1. Reference instability masks the per-cell change

ΔCq,control = 25 − 20 = 5;   ΔCq,X = 24 − 19 = 5
ΔΔCq = 0;   2−ΔΔCq = 1
MXMcontrol = 1 × 2 = 2

The target/H ratio is unchanged, while both target and H abundance per cell double. H is therefore unsuitable as an unvalidated invariant reference. Independent per-cell calibration restores the scale discarded by ratio normalization. Equal viable-cell sampling, matched extraction and reverse transcription, assay specificity and the stated amplification efficiencies are required. Mature RNA abundance alone cannot distinguish increased production from reduced RNA loss.

Q2. Recovery-corrected RNA production

Control: 1000.80 = 125 AU;   X: 1500.60 = 250 AU;   ratio = 250125 = 2

Under equal pulse duration, equivalent incorporation, matched extraction, proportional purification losses, linear detection and negligible loss during the pulse, the corrected signal supports approximately twofold gene-specific RNA production over the pulse. Unchanged long-term mRNA decay is not required for this pulse inference. The post-extraction spike corrects downstream purification yield; it cannot establish cellular 4sU uptake/incorporation, viability, extraction recovery, or equal target processing and detection. Those conditions must be checked independently or explicitly assumed.

This is not evidence that X binds a transcription factor or directly regulates a particular promoter or enhancer. Initiation, elongation, RNA processing, indirect signaling and treatment timing remain incompletely resolved. Comparable nascent-RNA detectability limits processing confounding in the idealized calculation; it does not establish unchanged splicing or RNA maturation in actual cells.

Q3. Inhibitor-derived half-lives do not identify native turnover

The 30% residual synthesis in treated cells violates a decay-only interpretation. Complete inhibition in control does not prove that cycloheximide leaves degradation or proliferation unchanged. Its apparent 2 h value therefore need not equal the independent 4 h total-loss half-life. Residual synthesis, inhibitor-induced changes in turnover or growth, and fitting a non-exponential trajectory could contribute to discrepancies; these data do not identify their individual effects. Global translation-inhibitor artifacts are documented in yeast, but their magnitude for R in human cells is unknown. [S4]

With constant post-inhibitor synthesis and clearance:   P(t)P(0) = a + (1 − a)e−kpostt,   a = SreskpostP(0)

If the pretreatment state is steady and Sres = 0.30 Spre, then a = 0.30 kpre/kpost, not necessarily 0.30. Neither constant residual synthesis throughout the chase nor unchanged post-inhibitor clearance is established. A unique native rate or an exact “corrected” half-life cannot be recovered from the supplied apparent half-life and residual-synthesis percentage. Panel D avoids global inhibition and separates the labeled cohort from new synthesis, but still requires its own recycling, recovery, population and kinetic assumptions.

At steady state, the inferred synthesis rates are approximately 0.173287 and 0.346574 relative protein units per cell per hour. They are model-derived fluxes, not direct synthesis measurements. Twofold synthesis and a 1.50-fold clearance factor reproduce threefold protein abundance. ε is a net effective output per mature mRNA; equality does not establish unchanged initiation, elongation, isoform composition, localization, cotranslational loss or every regulatory pathway. Compensating changes could yield the same aggregate value. The treated total-loss constant is two-thirds of control; the inverse ratio, 1.50, is the abundance multiplier at fixed synthesis. Claim A is false: degradation alone predicts 1.20, not 3. Claim B conflicts with the corrected pulse result under its assumptions. Claim C is supported conditionally by the supplied steady-state model. Claim D is unsupported: neither a direct transcriptional target nor a proteasomal route was established.

The two standalone 1.20 factors cannot be multiplied: both use the same control baseline and clearance terms add in the denominator. If one change is applied first, the second conditional factor is 1.25, giving 1.20 × 1.25 = 1.50. The intrinsic half-life ratio 8/6 ≈ 1.33 is not the degradation-only abundance ratio when growth dilution is present. A unique percentage attribution does not follow from this multiplicative reconciliation; any allocation requires an additional convention. These are model counterfactuals, not evidence that the biological mechanisms can be manipulated independently.

Q7. Experiments that resolve remaining mechanisms

Direct protein synthesis

Use a short stable-isotope amino-acid pulse with R-specific quantitative mass spectrometry, normalized to viable-cell number and independently calibrated mature target mRNA. Include matched pulse durations, precursor-pool enrichment, R-specific recovery standards, linear response and multiple pulse lengths to check early linearity and loss during labeling. Collect biological replicates and contemporaneous growth/viability measurements. The model predicts approximately twofold synthesis per cell and unchanged effective synthesis per mRNA. A discrepancy would motivate testing steady state, precursor incorporation, cohort representativeness and rapid protein loss before attributing a change to translation efficiency.

Endogenous cis-regulatory element perturbation

Perturb a candidate promoter or distal enhancer at the endogenous locus using CRISPR interference or targeted sequence editing, comparing vehicle and X in a factorial design. Use multiple independent guides or edits, non-targeting and nearby negative controls, an appropriate positive control, and confirmation of editing or local repression. Measure short-pulse nascent target RNA, mature RNA, cell number and viability. Analyze whether perturbation changes the treatment response, not merely basal expression; avoid a floor effect from silencing the gene completely. Independent elements and an orthogonal perturbation help test specificity. [S7]

Loss of the X response would support a requirement for the tested endogenous element in that context. It would not prove direct compound binding, identify a cognate transcription factor or exclude indirect signaling. An unchanged response could reflect an irrelevant element, incomplete perturbation, redundant enhancers or an alternative regulatory mechanism. Reporter activity alone would not establish endogenous necessity.

Protein-degradation pathway

Compare R pulse-chase kinetics under controlled proteasomal and lysosomal perturbations, with and without X. Verify pathway engagement with validated substrates, match growth and viability, and monitor R synthesis so abundance accumulation is not mistaken for blocked degradation. Orthogonal genetic perturbations strengthen inference. A pathway-dependent change in R clearance would support involvement, but drug interactions and stress responses would still prevent a direct-target claim without additional evidence.

Q8. Example scientific conclusion

Independent calibration indicates twofold mature target mRNA per cell despite an unchanged target/H ratio. Corrected nascent-RNA signal also doubles under comparable labeling and recovery assumptions, while protein abundance triples. Under the homogeneous first-order, steady-state model, the independent pulse-chase implies degradation half-lives of 6 and 8 h, approximately doubled protein synthesis and unchanged effective synthesis per mRNA. Increased synthesis and a 1.50-fold clearance contribution jointly account for the abundance difference; degradation alone does not. Residual synthesis and possible inhibitor perturbations prevent identification of native turnover from the cycloheximide chase. Direct transcriptional targets, RNA-processing mechanisms, translation steps and degradation pathways remain unresolved. These simulated point values have no biological replication or uncertainty estimates. A non-steady protein pool or a labeled cohort unrepresentative of bulk R would change the inferred synthesis rates.

Rubric V2.0: 40 points. This revision changes criterion weights and must not be used to regrade the historical V1 score without labeling a new retrospective analysis. What the historical pilot shows—and leaves unresolved

Claude recovered the principal V1 numerical results. The Q2 deduction concerned an unnecessary unchanged-mRNA-decay condition despite the stipulated negligible loss during the pulse. That criterion required judgment; the score is a historical grading decision, not a uniquely determined measure of performance.

The response also introduced an unjustified exact correction of the cycloheximide half-life and confused a sequential growth contribution with a standalone control-baseline effect. The original rubric did not assign separate penalties for these additions, so the recorded score is preserved. Correct central conclusions and unsupported extra calculations are reported separately; no new penalties or corrected model responses have been invented.

A single scaffolded trial cannot establish repeatability, graduate-level discrimination, general model ranking or benchmark validity. V2 reduces interpretive cues in the evidence, specifies counterfactual baselines and adds explicit reasoning criteria. Its difficulty and scoring reliability still require empirical evaluation and independent scientific review.

Future evaluation protocol

The versioned evaluation package contains an exact V2 solver prompt, separate answers and rubric, a run-record template and reproducible calculations. For each future model, freeze the prompt and rubric hashes, use fresh sessions, specify tool access and sampling settings, retain every submitted prompt and verbatim response, and record failures and exclusions. Predeclare models, repetitions and scoring rules; never select only the best response.

Keep answers and rubric outside the solver input. Score by criterion with evidence excerpts, record unsupported claims, and use blinded second review with documented adjudication when available. Report per-run scores and variability; do not infer population rankings from an isolated run. Tool-enabled and closed-book evaluations are separate conditions. No future runs or reviewers are represented as completed here.

Proposed V3 challenge (not administered). A separately versioned assessment could omit the supplied kinetic equations and itemized rate calculations, ask solvers to derive the per-cell mass balance, and request identification of missing measurements before mechanistic attribution.
